01 — Specify

Every number you need
to pick a camera

Chipset decides which analytics run on it. Field of view decides whether it covers the scene. Price decides whether it gets signed off. All three sit on the page you are already reading — no second tab, no spreadsheet.

FEATURE / 01

The whole catalogue in the toolbar

Search 341 models and 432 priced SKUs by name or Axis part number, from any tab. Every row carries its chipset, its list price and a heart that pins it to the top of every list in the extension. Click a model name, part number or category to copy it.

FEATURE / 02

The four categories you actually open

Pick your Axis product categories from the popup itself — + at the end of the quick-link row — or keep hearting tiles on axis.com; both write the same four shortcuts and stay in sync. Four is the cap, the same one axis.com uses, so the labels never squeeze to an ellipsis.

FEATURE / 03

Filter by chipset, sort by price or lens

Narrow to the ARTPEC or Ambarella generation your analytics need, then sort by price or by widest field of view. Favourites stay pinned on top whichever order you choose, and the filter applies to both the catalogue and the map results at once.

NEW IN v6.0

Will it get AXIS OS 13? Can it run deep learning?

Two questions that used to mean a support page and a datasheet, now answered in the badge row. OS 13 is resolved per model from Axis’s own Y2038 guidance — ARTPEC-6 products never make the jump, and where the published sources genuinely disagree the badge stays quiet rather than guessing. DLPU / MLPU comes from Axis’s own Product selector feed, because chipset generation is no shortcut: ARTPEC-7 alone spans deep learning, machine learning and no compute unit at all.

FEATURE / 04

Badges on axis.com itself

Every recognised product tile gets up to four badges — chipset, AXIS OS 13, DLPU / MLPU and price — on the Product Selector, search results, product and category pages, and inside the Compare Products table. Each of the four switches off independently in Settings.

FEATURE / 05

A filter panel the Product Selector never had

Multi-select chipset filtering, a required-angle slider, an ACAP dropdown that narrows to what CamStreamer supports, and cheapest-first series sorting — all applied live to the page, with your pinned categories and tools one click away at the top.

FEATURE / 06

Click to copy, exactly what you clicked

Part number into the quote, model name into the email, category into the spec — click the field you want and it is on the clipboard, with a checkmark to confirm. Multipack SKUs drop their (10 pcs) suffix so what you paste is the number Axis actually orders against.

FEATURE / 07

Speaks your language

Every string in the popup, the panel and the scanner is translated into twelve languages — English, Czech, Japanese, Spanish, German, French, Korean, Chinese, Swedish, Ukrainian, Arabic and Dutch. It picks one from your browser on first run; the flag row switches it any time.

FEATURE / 08

An IP Utility for Mac and Linux, finally

Axis only ever shipped AXIS IP Utility for Windows. Every Mac and Linux integrator has been stuck borrowing a laptop, running a VM, or guessing addresses.

This is the same job done in the browser, so it runs anywhere Chrome or Firefox does — macOS, Windows, Linux, ChromeOS. Type the subnet, press Scan, and watch devices appear as they answer: model, IP, serial, firmware and chipset, straight from the cameras over VAPIX, with no install and nothing leaving the machine.

02 — Coverage

Draw the coverage.
Get the cameras.

Drop a pin where the camera goes, drag the wedge over what it has to see, and the list underneath becomes the models that actually cover it — ranked by how tightly they fit, not by how wide they are.

  • PTZ pan counts, but honestly. A PTZ that only reaches your angle by panning sorts below everything that covers the wedge outright — it watches one direction at a time.
  • Tightest fit first. A 360° panoramic technically covers a 90° wedge; it is rarely the right answer. Results rank by least overkill, with price breaking ties.
  • Cameras only. Clips, licences, recorders and intercoms have no field of view, so they never appear — the catalogue lists stay cameras even when you sort by cheapest.
  • Same requirement everywhere. The wedge you draw and the Required FoV slider are one value, shared by the popup, the map and the Product Selector panel.
03 — Your network

The Axis devices
already on site

IP Utility Scanner

Type a subnet, press Scan, and get every Axis device that answers — model, IP, serial, firmware and chipset — with a CSV export for the handover document. Results stream in as the sweep runs, so you are not waiting on all 254 addresses to see the first camera.

  • Asks each address for its public device info over VAPIX basicdeviceinfo — no password needed on AXIS OS 10 and later.
  • Permission is requested for the one subnet you type, at the moment you scan — nothing is granted at install time.
  • Nothing leaves your computer. No server, no telemetry; the results live in local storage so reopening the popup shows the last scan instantly.
  • Presets for 192.168.0, 192.168.1, 10.0.0 and the factory-default .90 address.
04 — Fits in

Light or dark,
and twelve languages

The popup follows your choice, not your operating system’s — one click in the header. Every string is translated into twelve languages, with prices in EUR, USD, GBP or JPY, or hidden entirely when you are sharing your screen with a customer.

05 — Install

One click,
no developer mode

  1. 1Open the 4XS Toolbox listing on the Chrome Web Store.
  2. 2Click Add to Chrome, then confirm with Add extension. That is the whole install.
  3. 3Open the Product Selector — badges, the Filters panel and the field-of-view slider appear on their own. Tabs that are already open pick it up within a moment, no reload needed.
  4. 4Click the toolbar icon to search the catalogue, draw coverage on the map, or scan your network for Axis devices.
  5. 5Heart the product categories you work in on axis.com/products and they become buttons in the popup.

06 — Prices

Your price list,
not just mine

Drop in the monthly .xls

The bundled catalogue is the published AXIS Price List. When a new one lands in your inbox, drop the .xls on the settings page and every badge, every popup row and every map result uses your figures instead — no reinstall, no waiting for a release.

  • The standard AXIS Price List is recognised automatically — it reads the All products sheet, covering every category.
  • Any other spreadsheet can be mapped by hand, including telling it which currency the price column is in.
  • Nothing is uploaded anywhere except the FX rate lookup. Revert to bundled prices undoes it in one click.
07 — Where the data comes from

No magic,
no scraping you

PRICES

catalog-data.js holds one entry per SKU, extracted from the published AXIS Price List. EUR is the one defined list-price figure; USD, GBP and JPY are FX-derived from it and marked with a leading ~, so an approximation always looks like one.

CHIPSETS

chipset-data.js bundles a snapshot parsed from CamStreamer’s supported-hardware list — refreshed weekly by the background service worker, or on demand from the popup.

FIELD OF VIEW

fov-data.js carries each model’s minimum and maximum horizontal angle. A PTZ’s full pan range is treated as coverage only where that is honest — and a model with no FoV data is never counted as a match.

YOUR NETWORK

Scan results are read directly from the cameras over VAPIX and kept in local storage. Nothing is sent anywhere — there is no server behind this extension, no account and no analytics.
